          Proof of concept on real data: OpenStreetMap streets, building footprints, terrain (streets carved as flow paths; water goes around cells that are at least 75% built), and Project NOAH flood hazard as a reference layer. FABDEM heights can be 1–3 m off in dense cities, and this is a spread from measured points over terrain and buildings, not a hydraulic model. Each geotagged unit reports water depth at its own location; the model sets a local water surface there (ground + measured depth) and spreads it across the terrain only where topography allows, with a conservative decay away from the observation. No sensor reading, no modeled extent. Sensor readings are simulated — storm response is calibrated per site using its NOAH hazard class and local elevation. Not a forecast; not for emergency use.
