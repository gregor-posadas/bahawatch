BahaWatch · design draft · 26 Sept 2026

Babaha ba? Will it flood here in the next hour?

Every five minutes, for every place people follow, BahaWatch answers with one word: Oo (yes), Baka (maybe) or Hindi (no). The checks run top to bottom and the first match wins. Sensors outrank neighbours' reports, and the page never says "Hindi" on stale data.

Oo · square Baka · triangle Hindi · circle No fresh data · dashed Same shapes as the map markers, so the answer reads without colour.

The same rule in words

  1. If the newest sensor reading, report or rain figure is more than 20 minutes old, say Walang bagong datos (no fresh data). Stop.
  2. If a sensor here reads 5 cm or more, will reach 5 cm within 60 minutes at its current rate, or water from a connected sensor will arrive within 60 minutes, say Oo.
  3. If 3 or more "Yes" reports came from different phones within 1 km in the last hour: say Baka if a sensor within 500 m is dry and steady, otherwise say Oo.
  4. If heavy rain meets a flood-prone place, orange or red rain falls anywhere NOAH has mapped, 1–2 "Yes" reports came in, or a sensor shows 1–4 cm, say Baka.
  5. Otherwise, say Hindi.

What each input means

"Here"

The street the person picked, if it has a sensor. Otherwise within about 1 km of their phone's location, if they allow it. Otherwise the barangay they picked. No account needed; the choice stays on their phone.

Sensors

BahaWatch household units measuring water depth at the gate. 5 cm is where water stands on the street; the 60-minute look-ahead uses each sensor's rate of rise and the time water takes to travel between connected sensors.

Reports

One tap: "Is there flooding here?" Yes / No / Not sure. Counted per phone, not per tap, and only within 1 km and the last hour. An invisible bot check stops spam without logins.

Flood-prone places

UP Project NOAH flood hazard maps for 5-, 25- and 100-year storms, plus how low the place sits in the terrain.
